# Customer Segmentation & RFM Analysis — Identifying High-Value and At-Risk Customers

## 1. Business Problem

The company has a large number of customers and transaction records but lacks a clear understanding of customer purchasing behavior and value. Without customer segmentation, marketing efforts may be applied uniformly, making it difficult to identify high-value customers, loyal customers, and customers showing signs of potential inactivity.

The objective of this analysis is to segment customers based on their purchasing behavior using **RFM (Recency, Frequency, Monetary) analysis** and **K-Means clustering**. This approach will help identify distinct customer segments, understand their purchasing patterns, and develop targeted marketing strategies.

Therefore, this analysis aims to provide actionable insights to improve customer retention, strengthen customer loyalty, and increase overall revenue.

### Main Question

> How can we segment customers based on their **purchasing behavior** to identify high-value and at-risk customers, and what marketing strategies can be implemented to improve customer retention and increase revenue?

### Sub-Questions

#### Customer Value

* Which customers are the most valuable to the company?
* What percentage of total revenue is generated by high-value customers?
* What proportion of customers contributes to the majority of the company's revenue?

#### Customer Loyalty

* Which customers purchase most frequently and recently?
* Which customers demonstrate the strongest purchasing engagement?

#### Potential Inactivity

* Which customers show behavioral patterns associated with potential inactivity?
* Which customers have not purchased for an extended period?

#### Marketing

* Which customer segments should be targeted with loyalty incentives or promotions?
* Which customer segments should be targeted with personalized marketing campaigns to increase engagement and spending?
* Which customers should be targeted with re-engagement campaigns to reduce potential inactivity and improve retention?

## 2. Dataset Information

### Overview

This **Online Retail II** dataset contains transactions from a UK-based, registered, non-store online retailer between **01/12/2009 and 09/12/2011**. The company mainly sells unique all-occasion giftware, and many of its customers are wholesalers.

The dataset covers approximately two years of transactions, allowing us to analyze historical purchasing behavior and identify customer segments based on their purchasing patterns.

### Attribute Information

* **Invoice**: Invoice number. Nominal. A 6-digit integral number uniquely assigned to each transaction. If this code starts with the letter `C`, it indicates a cancellation.
* **StockCode**: Product item code. Nominal. A 5-digit code uniquely assigned to each distinct product.
* **Description**: Product item name.
* **Quantity**: Number of units purchased in the transaction. Numeric.
* **InvoiceDate**: Date and time when the transaction was generated.
* **Price**: Price per unit in British pounds (£). Numeric.
* **CustomerID**: Customer identification number. Nominal.
* **Country**: Country where the customer resides.

### Data Source

The dataset is available on [Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/tunguz/online-retail-ii/data).

## 5. RFM Analysis

RFM (**Recency, Frequency, Monetary**) analysis is a customer segmentation technique used to quantify customer purchasing behavior based on three dimensions:

* **Recency**: How recently a customer made a purchase. Customers with lower recency values have purchased more recently.
* **Frequency**: How frequently a customer makes purchases. Customers with higher frequency values purchase more often.
* **Monetary**: How much money a customer spends. Customers with higher monetary values generate more revenue.

The three metrics will be calculated at the customer level:

| Metric    | Definition                                     | Business Interpretation                         |
| --------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Recency   | Days since the customer's most recent purchase | Lower values indicate more recent activity      |
| Frequency | Number of purchases made by the customer       | Higher values indicate more frequent purchasing |
| Monetary  | Total amount spent by the customer             | Higher values indicate greater customer value   |

RFM analysis will provide a quantitative representation of customer purchasing behavior and will serve as the foundation for the clustering analysis.

## 6. RFM Feature Analysis

Before applying K-Means, we will analyze the distributions and relationships between **Recency, Frequency, and Monetary**.

This stage will help us:

* Understand the characteristics of the customer base.
* Identify skewed distributions and potential outliers.
* Examine relationships between RFM variables.
* Determine whether transformations or scaling are required before clustering.

The RFM variables will then be prepared as input features for the K-Means algorithm.

> **Note:** Customer segments will not be predefined at this stage. The final segments will be determined after analyzing the clusters generated by K-Means.

---

## 7. K-Means Clustering

K-Means clustering will be used to identify groups of customers with similar purchasing behavior based on their RFM characteristics.

### 7.1 Feature Scaling

Since K-Means is a distance-based algorithm, RFM variables will be standardized before clustering to prevent variables with larger numerical scales from disproportionately influencing the results.

### 7.2 Selecting the Number of Clusters

The appropriate number of clusters will be evaluated using:

* **Elbow Method**
* **Silhouette Score**

These methods will help determine an appropriate value of `K` while balancing cluster separation and model simplicity.

### 7.3 Model Training

The K-Means algorithm will then be trained using the scaled RFM features.

The model will assign each customer to the cluster whose centroid is closest to their RFM profile.

### 7.4 Cluster Assignment

Once the model has been trained, each customer will receive a cluster label representing their behavioral group.

These clusters will then be analyzed to understand the characteristics of each customer group.

---

## 8. Cluster Analysis & Customer Segmentation

After obtaining the clusters, we will analyze their RFM profiles to understand the behavioral characteristics of each group.

The analysis will include:

* Cluster size.
* Average Recency.
* Average Frequency.
* Average Monetary value.
* Revenue contribution by cluster.
* Customer distribution across segments.
* Comparison of RFM characteristics between clusters.

Based on these characteristics, each cluster will be assigned a **business-oriented interpretation**.

For example, potential segments may include:

* **High-Value Customers**
* **Loyal Customers**
* **Regular Customers**
* **At-Risk Customers**

However, these labels will be determined **after analyzing the actual cluster characteristics**, rather than being predefined beforehand.

> **Important:** An "At-Risk" segment represents customers whose historical purchasing behavior is associated with potential inactivity. K-Means itself does not predict churn.

## 12. Limitations

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting the results:

* The analysis is based on historical transaction data.
* RFM captures purchasing behavior but does not include other customer characteristics or interactions.
* K-Means requires the number of clusters to be selected beforehand.
* K-Means is sensitive to feature scaling and outliers.
* Customer segments represent behavioral patterns rather than predefined business categories.
* The analysis identifies customers with behavioral patterns associated with potential inactivity but does not directly predict churn.
